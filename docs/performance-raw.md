# Replication performance: measured figures

Measured end to end: the connector runs as a subprocess, exactly as the Airbyte
platform runs it, and RECORD messages are counted against wall-clock time. The
figures therefore include the SAP round trips, DuckDB, the CDK's serialization
and the write to stdout — not just the extraction.

Best of 1 run(s) per case. Generated by `bin/benchmark.py`.

| Case | Records | Time | Records/s | MB/s | Peak RSS |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| RFC DD02L, serial | 164,673 | 17.5s | 9,422 | 9.9 | 1,952 MB |
| RFC DD02L, 2 partitions | 164,673 | 111.0s | 1,484 | 1.6 | 1,330 MB |
| RFC DD02L, 4 partitions | 164,673 | 111.0s | 1,484 | 1.6 | 1,336 MB |
| RFC DD02L, 8 partitions | 164,673 | 110.5s | 1,491 | 1.6 | 1,335 MB |
| RFC DD02L, 2 of N columns, serial | 164,673 | 4.5s | 36,209 | 4.5 | 462 MB |
| RFC DD02L, 2 of N columns, 8 partitions | 164,673 | 5.9s | 28,042 | 3.5 | 400 MB |

## Effect of partitioning

| Partitions | Records/s | Speed-up vs serial |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 (serial) | 9,422 | 1.00x |
| 2 | 1,484 | 0.16x |
| 4 | 1,484 | 0.16x |
| 8 | 1,491 | 0.16x |

## Notes

- **RFC DD02L, serial** — baseline: one RFC_READ_TABLE call chain
- **RFC DD02L, 2 of N columns, serial** — differs from the serial baseline in the projection alone
- **RFC DD02L, 2 of N columns, 8 partitions** — narrow extracts are the regime where partitioning pays

## Environment

- Host: Linux-6.18.46-1-lts-x86_64-with-glibc2.44, 32 CPUs
- Python 3.13.12
- SAP: localhost client 001

The SAP system here is a single-container ABAP trial sharing a laptop with the
connector, so these are *relative* figures for comparing settings, not a
capacity statement about production hardware.
